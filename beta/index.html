motus caeli

Off: only the window and its light, as always. On: the window follows the real sky — cool at dawn, gold at noon, amber at evening, and at night a dark window with stars and candles in the nave. The hours let you preview it.

sonus

temperamentum

How the pipes are tuned against each other. In tono: centered on your home key, so home is always the purest place. In C, historice: tuned on C as the old organs were, so every key keeps its own color — F minor dark and aching, A minor warm. Werckmeister: Bach's era; near keys sweeter, distant ones tenser. Mesotonicus: Renaissance meantone; pure, glowing thirds, but a few remote chords turn sour. Pythagoricus: medieval pure fifths; open and austere. Aequalis: modern equal tuning.

diapason

The pitch of A. 415: baroque, darker. 466: Chorton, the high pitch of many German church organs in Bach's time.

ambitus

The life of the building around the organ. All off unless you raise them.

pedale

16′ adds a deep root under the chord; plena adds the 32′ sub-octave and a soft fifth. Now and then the pedal holds the home note while the chords wander above it.

angelus

While Cathedra is open and has been started, the bell rings the Angelus at 12:00 and 18:00: three strokes, three, three, then nine. The bell is tuned to the home note.
